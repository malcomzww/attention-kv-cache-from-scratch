## 3. Concurrency ceiling

Max concurrent sequences for Llama-3-8B, by context length and GPU.

Assumes 16 GiB weights and 2 GiB activation/framework overhead.

| context | A100-40G | A100-80G | H100-80G | H200-141G |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2,048 | 88 | 248 | 248 | 492 |
| 4,096 | 44 | 124 | 124 | 246 |
| 8,192 | 22 | 62 | 62 | 123 |
| 16,384 | 11 | 31 | 31 | 61 |
| 32,768 | 5 | 15 | 15 | 30 |

**This table is the answer to "why did the deployment cap context at 8k?"** The cap is not arbitrary: doubling context halves concurrency, which halves throughput and doubles cost per request. A context limit is a throughput decision wearing a correctness costume.


## 4. When the cache overtakes the weights

KV cache as a multiple of weight memory, Llama-3-8B (16 GiB weights).

| context | batch 1 | batch 8 | batch 32 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4,096 | 0.03x | 0.25x | 1.00x |
| 8,192 | 0.06x | 0.50x | 2.00x |
| 32,768 | 0.25x | 2.00x | 8.00x |
| 131,072 | 1.00x | 8.00x | 32.00x |

At long context and realistic batch the cache is several times the model. Any capacity plan that budgets only for weights is wrong by more than an order of magnitude.


## 5. KV-cache quantization

Llama-3-8B @ 8k on H100-80G. Halving KV bytes roughly doubles concurrency, which is why KV-cache quantization is often a bigger win than weight quantization at long context.

| KV dtype | bytes | KV @ 8k | max batch |
|---|---|---|---|
| fp16/bf16 | 2 | 1.00 GiB | 62 |
| fp8 | 1 | 0.50 GiB | 124 |
